Nº 02 — the basics

What is scrapbooking?

Scrapbooking is the craft of keeping memories on paper. You take photos and the small things you'd normally throw away (ticket stubs, letters, receipts, pressed flowers) and arrange them on a page with decoration and a few handwritten words.

Part diary, part art project, part time capsule. A finished scrapbook doesn't just show what happened. It shows how it felt.

Nº 03 — a little history

Older than you'd think

  • 1400s+
    Commonplace booksReaders copied quotes, recipes, poems and letters into one personal book.
  • 1800s
    Scraps for everyoneCheap printed pictures and cards spread, and people pasted them into albums: the first real "scrap" books.
  • 1873
    Mark Twain's inventionThe author patented a self-pasting scrapbook with pre-gummed pages. You just moistened them, no messy glue.
  • 1980
    The modern boomMarielen Christensen showed over 50 family albums at a records conference in Salt Lake City and helped start today's scrapbooking industry.
  • Today
    Junk journals & digital pagesFound-paper journals, digital kits and pages shared online.
Nº 04 — the kit

What you need

You don't need much to start. Most first pages are made with things already in a drawer. Here's the basic kit:

01Album
a journal, sketchbook or ring binder (rings let you add pages)
02Paper
cardstock, patterned paper, old book pages, maps
03Glue
glue stick, double-sided tape, photo corners
04Scissors
sharp scissors, a craft knife, or just tear it
05Decor
washi tape, stamps, stickers, wax seals, ribbon
06Memories
photos, tickets, letters, receipts, pressed flowers
Tip: look for acid-free or archival paper, glue and pens. They won't yellow or fade, so your pages last for decades.
Nº 05 — styles & techniques

Ways to build a page

LayeringStack papers of different sizes and textures behind a photo for depth.
Torn edgesTear paper towards you for a soft, white, feathery edge.
JournalingWrite the story: the date, the place, who was there, how it felt.
CollageMix print, paint, fabric and drawings, like the skull and rose here.
EphemeraSave the "throwaway" bits: labels, stamps, tickets, wrappers.
Junk journalingA looser book made from found and recycled papers.
Nº 06 — your first page

Getting started

  1. Pick one memoryA trip, a season, a person, a favourite day.
  2. Gather the piecesPhotos plus anything flat: tickets, notes, leaves, wrappers.
  3. Play before you glueMove things around and leave a little empty space.
  4. Stick it down in layersBackground paper first, then photos, then small details on top.
  5. Write a few wordsEven one line turns a page into a story.
